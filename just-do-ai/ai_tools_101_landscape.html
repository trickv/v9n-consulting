The AI Tools Landscape — Starter Edition

AI 101 — a plain-English map for the rest of us · updated 8 September 2026 · Patrick van Staveren
Everyday AI
Chat Assistants
"The one thing everybody should try first"
Best for: Everyone — writing, research, brainstorming, explaining things, second opinions
★
ChatGPT web · phone · desktop
The one your neighbor has heard of. Type a question, get an answer — plus voice conversation, image generation, file uploads, and Projects to keep related chats together. It remembers what you've told it before.
Free · Go $8/mo · Plus $20/mo · Pro $100–200/mo
★
Claude web · phone · desktop
Anthropic's assistant. The favorite for writing that sounds human, working through long documents, and thinking carefully about a problem. Artifacts turn its answers into live documents and mini-apps you can use right in the chat.
Free · Pro $20/mo · Max from $100/mo · Pro unlocks Cowork
Google Gemini web · phone · in Google apps
Google's assistant, already sitting inside Gmail, Docs and Android. The easiest starting point if your business already runs on Google Workspace, because it can see your mail and files without any setup.
Free · AI Plus $4.99/mo · AI Pro $19.99/mo · AI Ultra $99.99/mo
★
Gemini Notebook web · phone
The old NotebookLM, renamed in 2026. You give it your own documents — contracts, manuals, research, meeting notes — and it answers ONLY from those, with citations. It will also turn them into a podcast-style audio or video summary.
Generous free tier · Included in Google AI plans · Best for 'what's in my own files?'
Perplexity web · phone · browser
A search engine that answers in sentences and shows its sources. Reach for it when you want current, checkable facts rather than a conversation. Its Comet browser puts the same assistant alongside whatever page you're reading.
Free · Pro $20/mo · Comet browser now free on Mac, Windows, iOS & Android
Microsoft Copilot web · Windows · in Office
Free chat on the web and built into Windows. The paid version's real value is inside Word, Excel and Outlook — drafting documents, summarizing mail threads, and building spreadsheet formulas for you.
Free chat · Paid features bundled into Microsoft 365 consumer plans (~$20/mo)
Grok web · phone · on X
xAI's assistant, wired into X (Twitter) and known for a looser, more opinionated tone and real-time posts. Fine as a chat assistant; the more interesting piece is its Grok Bot agent, further down this page.
Free tier · SuperGrok tiers from ~$10/mo · Bundled with X Premium
How to choose
Honestly? Any of them. They are far more alike than different, and the leader changes every few months. Pick the one that fits where you already work — Google Workspace shop, use Gemini; Microsoft Office shop, use Copilot — or just try ChatGPT and Claude side by side for a week on real work and keep whichever one you'd miss.
On Your Computer
AI That Can Actually Do Things
"Same assistant — but now it can open your files and use your apps"
Best for: Once chat feels normal and you're tired of copying and pasting into it
★
Claude Desktop Mac · Windows app
The download-and-install version of Claude. Unlike the website, it can read and write files in folders you approve, and one-click extensions connect it to Google Calendar, Slack, Notion, GitHub and more. It asks permission before touching anything.
Free to install · Same account as claude.ai · macOS & Windows
★
Claude Cowork desktop · web · mobile
Hand Claude a whole job instead of a question — 'reconcile these invoices,' 'review these contracts,' 'build the monthly report from this folder.' It works through your files and connected apps step by step, keeps going after you close the laptop, and shows its work. No coding involved.
Requires a paid plan (Pro $20/mo and up) · Built into the Claude desktop app
ChatGPT desktop app Mac · Windows app
One app with three modes: Chat (the usual), Work (hand it a task and get back a finished doc, spreadsheet or deck), and Codex (the coding one). The separate Codex desktop app was folded into this in July 2026 — you don't need to hunt for it anymore.
Free to install · All three modes on every plan, including Free
Comet browser web browser
Perplexity's web browser with an assistant riding shotgun. It reads the page you're on, summarizes it, compares options across tabs, and can fill in forms and click through routine tasks for you. Went free in March 2026.
Free · Mac, Windows, iOS & Android · Pro $20/mo for heavier use
And many more: Claude Code, Gemini in Chrome, Copilot in Windows …
The one habit that matters here
These apps ask before they read a file, send a message, or spend money — and it is tempting to click "allow" on autopilot. Read the request. Start them on a copy of a folder rather than the real one, and give them access to the smallest slice of your business that gets the job done.
Build Without Code
Describe It, Get an App
"Type what you want in plain English — get a real, working website or app"
Best for: Anyone with an idea and no developer: internal tools, booking forms, calculators, simple sites
★
Lovable web
The friendliest starting point. Describe your app in a chat, watch it appear, then ask for changes in plain English. The database, logins and hosting are all handled for you — you never see a server or write a line of code.
Free tier (5 builds/day) · Pro ~$25/mo · Publish with one click
★
Base44 web
Owned by Wix. Same idea as Lovable — prompt in, working app out, with the database and user logins included. Widely rated the most beginner-proof of the bunch, and it has a genuinely free forever tier to experiment on.
Free forever tier · Starter $20/mo · Builder $50/mo · Pro $100/mo
Bolt web
By StackBlitz. Builds sites, apps and prototypes with hosting, database, logins and custom domains built in. Slightly more of a tinkerer's tool than Lovable — a little more power, a little more to look at.
Free tier · Pro $25/mo · Teams $30/user/mo
Figma Make web
Turns a description — or a design you already have in Figma — into a working app. The obvious pick if your business already designs in Figma, and an odd door to walk through if it doesn't.
Included in paid Figma plans
Replit web
An AI agent that builds and publishes your app, wrapped in a real programmer's workspace. It will do the whole job for you, but you'll be looking at actual code the entire time — which some people find motivating and others find alarming.
Credit-based · Core $20/mo · Pro $100/mo · A step toward the developer world
And many more: v0, Google AI Studio, Emergent …
What to expect, honestly
You will get something impressive in an afternoon. Getting it to 90% done is genuinely easy now; the last 10% — payments, real customer data, security, the weird edge case — is still where people get stuck, and it's the right moment to bring in a human. Start with something low-stakes and internal before you build anything a customer touches.
Always-On Agents
AI That Keeps Working After You Close the Laptop
"Give it a job, walk away, come back to finished work"
Best for: The frontier — overnight research, inbox triage, recurring chores. Newest and roughest category here.
Everything above waits for you to open it. These keep running on their own — on a schedule, or until a long job is done. The catch is where the agent lives. Some run on the vendor's computers, so you just sign up. The ones marked † are free, open-source software that has to run on a computer that never sleeps — which means either renting one from a hosting company for a few dollars a month and following setup instructions, or paying a managed host to do that part for you.
★
Gemini Spark nothing to install
Google's 24/7 assistant. It runs on Google's servers, so it works with your laptop shut. You hand it jobs by email, it browses the web through Chrome, and it acts across Gmail, Calendar, Docs and Drive. The easiest way to see what an always-on agent actually feels like.
Included with Google AI Pro $19.99/mo · 150+ countries — not the EU, UK or Switzerland
Lindy nothing to install
The closest thing to hiring an AI assistant for a small business. Describe the job — 'sort my inbox and draft replies in my voice' — and it builds the agent for you. Connects to Gmail, Outlook, Slack, HubSpot, Salesforce and a hundred others.
7-day trial, no free plan · Plus $49.99/mo · Pro $99.99/mo · Usage-metered
Grok Bot nothing to install
xAI's always-on teammates, launched August 2026. Each one gets its own cloud computer with a browser and files, signs into your apps the way a person would, and grinds through long jobs — 'research these twenty companies overnight.' Still in beta.
Not sold on its own — bundled into xAI's top SuperGrok tier and premium Cursor plans (~$200/mo territory) · Verify before buying
Hermes Agent † hosted or self-hosted
Free, open-source, and it teaches itself: after each task it writes down a reusable skill, so it gets better at your work over time. Reaches you over Telegram, Discord, Slack or email, and can run on a schedule. The makers now offer Hermes Cloud, a hosted version — pick a name and a model and it's online, no server required.
Hermes Cloud (preview): $10 minimum credit · Self-hosted: free software + ~$5–7/mo server + model usage
OpenClaw † self-hosted
Free, open-source personal assistant you talk to through WhatsApp or Telegram, with voice, memory and skills. Everything stays on your own machine, which is the appeal. The official setup assumes a Mac you leave running, some terminal commands, and a spare phone number for the assistant — so most newcomers use a managed host instead.
Software free · Managed hosting from ~$4–10/mo (OneClaw, Agent 37, Hostinger) · Plus model usage
And many more: Zapier Agents, Microsoft Copilot agents, Perplexity Computer ($200/mo), Claude Cowork …
† What "you'll need a hosting company" really means
The software is free; the computer it lives on is not. In practice you either rent a small server from a hosting company like Hetzner, DigitalOcean or Hostinger (roughly $5–10 a month) and follow a setup guide that involves the terminal and pasting in API keys — or you pay a managed host (OneClaw, Agent 37 and others, from about $4–10 a month) that does the setup for you in a few clicks. Either way you also pay the AI model itself, usually a few dollars to $15 a month for personal use. This is the one category on this page where a weekend of help from a technical friend pays for itself.
You do not need all of these. Pick one chat assistant and use it every day for two weeks — that single habit is worth more than a shelf full of subscriptions.
Prices and features change monthly. Anything with a † needs a computer that never sleeps, which means paying a hosting company and doing real setup — happy to help with that part.
